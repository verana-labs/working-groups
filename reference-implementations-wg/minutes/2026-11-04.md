# Reference Implementation WG — 2026-11-04

## Agenda

1. Playground wipe, redeployment and documentation
2. End-of-year roadmap, mainnet target and V5 priority
3. Spec-first rule and scope reduction (visualizer)
4. Post-V5: cloud (SaaS) project and production/infrastructure skills
5. Deployment rules (devnet vs testnet) and per-component responsibles
6. Pull request review, merge and release practice; notifications
7. Hologram status and end-to-end testing
8. Post-quantum verifiable credentials status

## Discussion

**Playground.** Fabrice Rochette said he would wipe the playground and redeploy it cleanly with reorganised demos and workflows, and publish documentation for people wanting to create further demos. He aimed to finish the same day, as a new demo for France (nuclear plants) is due Friday and he is travelling afterwards.

**Roadmap and V5.** Fabrice asked for a roadmap for the end of the year, mentioning customer requirements and the wish to explore starting the mainnet at the beginning of 2027, while acknowledging it may not be achievable. The priority is V5, needed to launch with the Colombian customers, with work expected in VS Agent, the blockchain, the indexer and other components. Only the VPR and Verifiable Trust specifications have been moved to V5 so far; he asked everyone to read them properly and raise concerns within a week, after which they would be validated. The agreed sequence is blockchain first, then the other components, each requiring its own V5 specification. Fabrice argued that with AI the value now lies in the specification rather than the code, so specification work should be shared rather than left to him and Ariel Gentile. He asked for human review of the specs, noting that AI can find inconsistencies but cannot judge whether a design is good.

**Scope and the visualizer.** Fabrice stated that anything not specified should not be developed, and that work on the visualizer stops for now. Ariel Gentile added that the visualizer upgrade turned out not to be needed and that the graph already covers part of its functionality, suggesting the missing parts could be folded into the graph; each additional module is another component to keep in sync, secure and maintained. Maxime Mansiet took responsibility for having updated the visualizer without checking first.

**Cloud project and infrastructure skills.** Fabrice described a future SaaS "cloud" project allowing companies to deploy verifiable services in one click instead of self-hosting; it is not yet defined and would be specification-based. He also noted the team has no expert in deploying production-grade, high-availability blockchain-based software and asked for names of candidates to expand the team early next year.

**Deployment and responsibles.** Ariel restated that dev versions go to devnet without approval, while testnet waits for stable releases. Maxime argued that whatever reaches main should be production-ready, reviewed and tested, with the merger taking responsibility. Ariel and Maxime agreed the rules can depend on component criticality: the frontend and graph can be deployed quickly and rolled back, whereas a bad indexer deployment on testnet would require hours of resync. Fabrice argued that naming a responsible per project would speed up merging and deployment rather than slow it; approved PRs should be merged unless there is a good reason (e.g. stacking PRs on a branch). Ariel clarified that "responsible" means responsible for releases and overall status, not the only person allowed to merge. Proposed responsibles: 
- VS Agent and mediator/Hologram – Ariel
- node and indexer – Pratik Kumar
- frontend – Maxime
- graph – Pratik (suggested by Fabrice).

Ariel suggested keeping actual testnet deployment with Andres Vallecilla and himself because of cluster access, with responsibles opening the deploy PRs. Andres is the responsible within the team for the infrastructure in general (devnet/testnet, Hologram dev/prod).

**Review practice.** Maxime noted PRs sitting approved but unmerged and several frontend releases left open because it was unclear who should cut them; he and Andres both raised that re-review is often not requested after comments are addressed. Maxime said he had opened a PR on verana-deploy to deploy main automatically to devnet while limiting exposure of Kubernetes variables, and asked Ariel to review it. The group discussed Discord notifications for PR events; Fabrice and Maxime were sceptical about adding noise, preferring personal tooling, pings for urgent items, and a later rework of the Discord channels.

**Hologram.** Maxime said the new UI/layout issue must be finished before starting the end-to-end issues Ariel opened. Ariel said the end-to-end automation is the basis for measuring reliability and React Native performance bottlenecks.

**Post-quantum.** Martin Evain described his work implementing ML-DSA in Askar for use via Credo; the first module part is merged and the Python API is done, with questions to review with Ariel, then the JavaScript interface and Credo integration. Ariel outlined three fields: blockchain accounts (Cosmos SDK reportedly supports ML-DSA 44, maturity unclear), verifiable credentials, and securing did:webvh logs, which would require spec evolution beyond Ed25519. He noted most libraries carry "use at your own risk" warnings and said he would ask the other Askar maintainers for a new release keeping the feature experimental/optional. Fabrice asked whether Cosmos SDK addresses and consensus are both covered; Pratik said he would check.

## Decisions

- Components that are not specified are not developed; visualizer work is stopped and its functionality may be absorbed by the graph.
- V5 specifications (VPR, Verifiable Trust) are to be reviewed by everyone within one week, then validated; blockchain is implemented first, then other components, each with its own V5 spec.
- A responsible is named per component: VS Agent, mediator and Hologram – Ariel; node and indexer – Pratik; frontend – Maxime; graph – Pratik.
- Testnet deployments continue to be carried out by Ariel and Andres; responsibles open the deployment PRs when a component is ready.
- No centralised Discord notifications for PR reviews; review requests and personal tooling are used instead.
